## Pre-swelling

A carvel hull relies on the planks swelling with water to close up the seams. Before launch, you soak the hull for days — running hoses over the topsides and filling the interior — to let the wood take up as much water as it can before it hits the harbor. The alternative is launching dry and watching the ocean pour in through every seam.

## Pump!!!

Every wooden boat leaks on first launch. The planks haven't fully swelled, the caulking hasn't compressed, and the seams are still finding their shape. You plan for it — bilge pumps running, someone watching around the clock — but it's still unnerving to see water pouring into a boat you spent years building.
