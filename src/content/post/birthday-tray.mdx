My mom's birthday was coming up and I had a piece of figured black walnut crotch that was too good to leave in the lumber pile. Crotch wood comes from where the trunk forks into two branches — the grain swirls and compresses as it navigates the split, producing figure that straight lumber can't match.

I resawed the piece and book-matched the two halves, opening them like a book so the figure mirrors across the center line. A strip of maple runs down the joint between the two halves — partly decorative, partly to give the glue line something to hide behind.

The crack was there from the start — crotch wood is under internal stress, and when you resaw it the tension releases unevenly. Rather than try to glue it shut and hope, I cut in a bow tie (butterfly key) across the crack to lock it in place. The bow tie is maple to match the center strip.

I framed the panel in walnut with handle cutouts routed into the short sides and corner splines for reinforcement. The whole thing was finished with Tried & True Original wood finish — a linseed oil and beeswax blend. It's food-safe and brings out the depth of walnut figure without building up a plastic-looking film.
