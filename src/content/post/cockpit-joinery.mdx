The cockpit is where you spend most of your time on a sailboat. It's where you steer, trim sails, eat lunch, and watch the sun go down. On a boat built in the late 1920s, the cockpit joinery — the bulkhead, thwart, seats, and trim — has been doing that job for nearly a century. This post covers three sub-projects that put Ariadne's cockpit back together: rebuilding the companionway bulkhead, replacing the thwart, and trimming out the companionway opening.

## Replacing the companionway bulkhead

The original companionway bulkhead is a frame-and-panel construction in mahogany — two halves that bolt together around the centerline, with raised panels set into mortise-and-tenon frames. It's gorgeous joinery. It's also the primary structural connection between the deck, the cabin house sides, and the hull. When it's soft or loose, the entire upper structure flexes.

We removed the entire bulkhead and brought it to the shop. Several of the frame members had rot or damage where fasteners had corroded, so we milled new pieces from matching mahogany stock and spliced them in, keeping as much of the original as possible.

Beautiful as the mahogany is, frame-and-panel construction has an inherent weakness: the panels float in their grooves, and the joints between rails and stiles can work loose over time. The bulkhead looked great, but it still wasn't rigid enough to do its structural job.

The solution was to face the entire inboard side of the bulkhead with epoxy-sealed marine plywood. The plywood turns the bulkhead from a decorative partition into a structural diaphragm — a single rigid plane that ties the cabin sides to the deck and resists the twisting forces that a hull generates under sail. It's invisible from the cockpit side, where all you see is the original mahogany.

## Replacing the thwart

The thwart is the structural crosspiece that spans the cockpit from rail to rail. It carries the mainsheet block — all the loads from the mainsail pass through this single timber — and it supports the forward edges of the cockpit seats on either side. The original was worn and no longer up to the job.

Before we could fit the new thwart, there was some demolition. A previous owner had installed automotive leaf spring steel as floor timber bracing — a creative improvisation, but not one that belonged in a wooden boat. The springs had rusted badly and were doing more harm than good.

The cockpit seats attach to the thwart at the forward end and land on the covering boards at the rails. Each seat section was test-fitted and trimmed to bear against the inside face of the planking.

<CaptionedImage
    src={img_2418}
    alt="Cockpit seat test-fitted"
    caption="A cockpit seat section test-fitted against the planking. The seat bears on the inside face of the hull and ties into the thwart, stiffening the topsides."
/>

With the thwart installed and the seats fitted, the cockpit went from a collection of loose pieces to an integrated structure. The thwart ties the two sides of the hull together, the seats stiffen the topsides, and the whole assembly works as a unit. Underneath the seats there's a surprising amount of structure — cleats, blocking, and knee braces that lock everything in place so nothing can shift regardless of how the boat moves. It all has to stay put when the boat is heeled over and slamming into waves.

## Finishing the companionway trim

With the bulkhead rebuilt and the thwart in place, the last step was trimming out the companionway opening — the transition between the cockpit and the cabin below. This means a step at the base of the opening, trim pieces to hold the cockpit sole, and guides for the drop boards that close the companionway when the boat is underway or at anchor.

<CaptionedImage
    src={img_2679}
    alt="Companionway step"
    caption="The new mahogany companionway step, fitted between the varnished seat sides. A small detail, but it defines the threshold between cockpit and cabin."
/>

<CaptionedImage
    src={img_2680}

/>

Small details, but they make the difference between a boat that's being worked on and one that's ready to use.
